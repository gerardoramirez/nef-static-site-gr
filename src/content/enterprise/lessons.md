## The Hard Truths

### 1. Engineers overbuild

My first instinct is always to make it more robust, more scalable, more configurable. Customers don't care about any of that until they care about it — which is only after they've decided to pay you. Ship something rough and learn.

### 2. Distribution is harder than the product

I've seen mediocre products win because the founder was relentless about distribution, and great products fail because they assumed people would find them. Marketing isn't optional — it's the job.

### 3. "AI" is both a door opener and a trust barrier

Saying you use AI gets you in the door. But it also raises immediate questions: "Is my data safe?", "What happens when it's wrong?", "Who's responsible?" Have clear answers ready.

### 4. Pricing too low is a signal problem

Charging $19/month for something that replaces 10 hours of work doesn't just leave money on the table — it makes buyers suspicious that something is wrong with it.

## What's Working

- Starting conversations with ROI framing, not tech framing
- Weekly iterations based on real user feedback
- Keeping the tech stack boring and reliable (not chasing new models every week)

## What I'm Still Figuring Out

- Scaling customer acquisition beyond warm referrals
- Building in public without giving away competitive angles
- Knowing when to double down vs. when to pivot
