## Finding the Right Angle

The biggest mistake I see engineers make when trying to build an AI business is leading with the technology. Customers don't buy technology — they buy outcomes. The framing has to start with the problem.

## Positioning Principles

**Niche down first.** It's tempting to build something general. General solutions compete with everyone. Specific solutions for specific industries can charge more and win more.

**Outcome-based pricing.** When AI automates something that used to take hours, pricing by the hour makes no sense. Pricing by the outcome — the lead found, the document reviewed, the ticket resolved — aligns value and revenue.

**Human in the loop.** Fully automated AI is a hard sell in high-stakes domains. Positioning the AI as an expert assistant, not a replacement, lowers the barrier to adoption.

## Target Customer Profiles

- **SMBs with repetitive back-office work** — High volume, low margin tasks that AI can automate at a fraction of the cost
- **Professional services firms** — Legal, accounting, consulting — places where knowledge work is billable and AI can multiply capacity
- **Internal tools buyers at mid-market companies** — Teams that can't afford enterprise software but have engineering resources to integrate APIs

## Current Thinking

I'm focusing on the SMB market first — faster sales cycles, less procurement friction, and real pain points that are underserved by the big players.
